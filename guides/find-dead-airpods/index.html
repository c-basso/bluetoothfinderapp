How to Find Dead AirPods

Last updated November 25, 2026

Quick answer

You can't track a dead AirPod live, but you can still find it. Open Find My and note the last location and time, even if it says Offline: that's roughly where the battery ran out. Turn on Notify When Found (and Mark As Lost on supported models) so your iPhone alerts you the moment they get any charge. Search that spot by hand, starting where dead AirPods usually end up: couch and car-seat gaps, pockets, laundry, under the bed. Then re-scan with a Bluetooth finder a few times a day, because a resting battery can recover enough charge to broadcast briefly.

How to Tell If Your AirPods Are Dead

Before you change strategy, confirm the battery is actually the problem. Dead AirPods show the same handful of signs:

  • Find My shows them as Offline or "No location found", with only a past time and place.
  • Play Sound fails or never starts.
  • They don't appear in your iPhone's Bluetooth list or in the Batteries widget.
  • A Bluetooth scan in the room where you last used them finds nothing.

Dead or Just Out of Range?

If Find My shows a live location or a recent time like "Now" or "2 minutes ago", they aren't dead — use Play Sound and a Bluetooth scan right away. If the last time is hours old, assume the battery ran out around then, and treat that place and time as your search zone.

A single earbud dies faster than one sitting in its case, so it's common for one AirPod to be dead while the other still shows up. If that's your situation, the one-lost-AirPod guide below walks through finding the missing side.

Dead AirPod Out of the Case, or Inside a Dead Case?

A loose earbud that died out of its case is the common case. It can't play a sound, so work from Find My's last location and the time it went offline, then search the places listed below. Earbuds are small and usually end up within a few meters of where you last wore them.

AirPods sitting inside a closed case usually don't show up in a Bluetooth scan, even when charged. On AirPods Pro 2 and later and AirPods 4 with Active Noise Cancellation, the case appears in Find My on its own and has a speaker you can ring, but only while the case has charge. If the case is dead too, nothing transmits: use the last location from Find My and search by hand.

The Honest Physics of a Dead AirPod

Every method of finding AirPods — Find My, Precision Finding, sound alerts, Bluetooth scanning — depends on the earbuds transmitting a radio signal. A dead battery means no transmission, and no transmission means nothing to detect. Any app that claims to locate a completely dead AirPod live is overpromising.

That doesn't mean the search is hopeless. It means the strategy changes from tracking a signal to narrowing the physical search area — and being ready to catch the signal if it comes back.

Turn On Lost Mode So You Know When They Wake Up

Dead AirPods can't be tracked, but Find My can tell you the moment they come back. Open Find My → Devices → your AirPods: when AirPods that were offline reconnect, Apple sends a notification to the devices they're paired with. On AirPods Pro, AirPods Max and AirPods (3rd generation) and later you can also Mark As Lost, which adds your contact details for whoever finds them.

On models with the Find My network turned on, the last location can stay useful for up to 24 hours after they last connected — another reason to start searching early rather than waiting for a recharge that may never come.

Three Things That Actually Work

First, the last known location. If Find My was set up, it recorded where the AirPods were last online. That pin is your search zone — go there and search methodically.

Second, residual charge. "Dead" batteries often recover a small amount of voltage after resting, and AirPods returned to a charged case even briefly will broadcast again. Scanning the search zone with a Bluetooth finder every few hours costs nothing and regularly pays off.

Third, probability. Dead AirPods are found in the same places over and over: laundry (check the machine drum), couch and car-seat gaps, jacket and bag pockets, under beds, and inside shoes. Search these first in your zone.

Dead Earbuds That Aren't AirPods

The same physics applies to Galaxy Buds, Sony, Jabra, Beats and every other wireless earbud: once the battery is empty, nothing transmits. Most non-Apple earbuds have no last-location feature at all, so the last place you used them and a periodic Bluetooth scan are your only leads — the lost-earbuds guide covers brand-specific apps that may help.

The Recovery Routine (15 Minutes a Day)

  1. Pin the last known location

    Open Find My → Devices → your AirPods. Note the timestamp and address of the last location, even if it says Offline. If your model supports it, turn on Mark As Lost so you're notified the moment they reconnect.

  2. Scan the zone with Find Bluetooth Device

    At the location, run a Bluetooth scan. If any charge remains and the buds are out of the case, they'll appear in the list — follow the hot–cold guidance immediately.

  3. Do the physical sweep

    No signal? Search high-probability spots: cushions, pockets, laundry, under furniture, car seat rails, bag linings.

  4. Re-scan on a schedule

    Repeat the scan morning and evening for a few days. Brief battery recovery or someone moving the buds can put a signal back on the air.

  5. Be ready for next time

    Keep a Bluetooth finder installed and enable Find My separation alerts. Devices caught while still broadcasting take minutes to find instead of days.

Catch the signal the moment it comes back

Keep Find Bluetooth Device installed and scan your search zone daily — and next time, catch your devices while they're still broadcasting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will Find My show my AirPods if they're dead?
It shows where they were last online, with a timestamp — not where they are now. Dead AirPods can't play a sound or update their location until they get some charge, so search around that last location and re-scan with a Bluetooth finder in case they wake up.
Can I find a dead AirPod in my house?
Yes, but by searching, not by signal. If Find My's last location is your home, the earbud is usually within a few meters of where you last wore it. Check couch and bed gaps, the laundry basket and washing machine drum, coat pockets and the floor along your usual routes. Scan with a Bluetooth finder each morning and evening in case it briefly wakes up.
Will I get a notification when my dead AirPods turn back on?
Yes, if they're linked to your Apple Account in Find My: when offline AirPods reconnect, Apple notifies the devices they're paired with. Turning on Mark As Lost (on supported models) also shows your contact details to whoever finds them.
What if my AirPods and the case are both dead?
Then nothing is transmitting, so neither Find My nor any app can locate them right now. Find My still shows where the case or AirPods were last online. Search there, and keep Notify When Found on: if someone charges the case, you'll get an alert with the new location.
How do I know if my AirPods are dead or just out of range?
Check Find My: if it shows Offline with an old timestamp, the battery is probably dead. If it shows a recent time, they're likely just out of range — move around that location and scan; they'll appear the moment you're within about 10–20 meters.
Can a dead AirPod be tracked?
Not live. A dead AirPod emits no Bluetooth signal, so no app — Find My included — can follow it in real time. Find My can show where it was last online, and a Bluetooth finder will detect it again the moment it regains any charge.
How long until lost AirPods go dead?
An earbud out of its case lasts up to roughly 5–7 hours of standby depending on model and battery health. Inside a charged case they stay topped up for weeks — but a closed case doesn't broadcast, so search for the case by location, not signal.
Is it worth scanning if they've been dead for days?
Yes, occasionally. If anyone returns the buds to a case with charge, or residual voltage briefly revives one, a scan will catch it. A 30-second scan of the search zone costs nothing.
